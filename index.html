About

Engineering Focus & Architecture

I'm a Senior Backend Developer at BigYellowFish Technologies, working on the backend systems that power the BigYellow platform. My work involves building APIs, working with databases (SQL), integrating services, and making sure things continue to work as expected in production.

I enjoy the problem-solving side of backend engineering — understanding why something isn't working, figuring out where things can be improved, and finding practical solutions without making systems unnecessarily complicated. Whether it's debugging an issue, improving a slow query, or working on a new feature, I like understanding the problem before jumping into a solution.

Over time, I've come to appreciate that good backend engineering is about more than writing code. It's about making thoughtful decisions, keeping systems maintainable, and considering what happens after a feature goes live. That's the approach I try to bring to my work every day.

Approach

Core Engineering Principles

01

Diagnose with Evidence

Read the structured logs and inspect execution plans with EXPLAIN ANALYZE before changing code. Assumptions waste time; metrics and telemetry pinpoint root causes.

02

Design for Maintainability

Code is written once but read hundreds of times. Clear abstractions, domain boundary isolation, predictable CQRS flows, and consistent naming always outlast clever shortcuts.

03

Understand Database Internals

Understand index cardinality and access paths before tuning. Index writes have a cost; transactions must be explicit and bounded to preserve data integrity.

04

Observability is Part of Shipping

Structured logging, meaningful latency metrics, and actionable alert routes belong in the pull request with the feature, not as an afterthought during an outage.

05

Automate the Repeatable

If an operational or deployment task has been executed manually three times, it belongs in an automated CI/CD pipeline or script. Toil compounds; automation scales.

06

Protect Data Integrity

Transactions, foreign keys, idempotency keys, and versioned Liquibase migrations are non-negotiable. Data corruption is infinitely harder to resolve than a failed build.
